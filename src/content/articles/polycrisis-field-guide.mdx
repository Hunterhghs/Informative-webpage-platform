## The idea in one paragraph

A **polycrisis** is a set of crises that interact, so that the combined harm is greater than the sum of the parts. The crises do not simply happen at the same time. They share causes, spill into one another and hit the same weak points. The practical consequence is that managing each risk on its own will underestimate the danger.

<Callout type="definition" title="Polycrisis">
A condition in which multiple crises interact through shared causes or knock-on effects, so that the total harm exceeds the sum of each crisis taken alone.
</Callout>

## Five levers that build resilience

<Cards
  cols={3}
  items={[
    { label: 'A', title: 'Substitutes', text: 'A second way to meet a need when the usual one fails, such as rooftop solar during a blackout.' },
    { label: 'B', title: 'Decentralisation', text: 'Many small units that fail one at a time, instead of one large system that fails all at once.' },
    { label: 'C', title: 'Speed of response', text: 'Information and resources that move faster than the shock spreads, such as early-warning alerts.' },
    { label: 'D', title: 'Learning curves', text: 'Technologies that get cheaper as more people use them, so each wave of adoption makes the next easier.' },
    { label: 'E', title: 'Household buffers', text: 'Savings, insurance and mobile payments that let families absorb a shock without lasting harm.' },
  ]}
/>

## Common questions

<FAQ
  items={[
    { q: 'Is a polycrisis just several crises at once?', a: 'No. The test is interaction. Crises that happen to coincide but do not affect one another are a coincidence. A polycrisis exists when they feed one another or exploit the same weak points.' },
    { q: 'Is the idea new?', a: 'The word dates to the 1990s. What is new is how widely it is used, after the overlapping shocks of the early 2020s.' },
    { q: 'Is it only a fashionable label?', a: 'It can be, when used loosely. Used carefully, it shifts attention from individual risks to the links between them, which is where much of the damage is done and where cheaper interventions often lie.' },
    { q: 'What should a government do differently?', a: 'Stress-test plans against several shocks at once, map shared dependencies, and invest in buffers that protect more than one system at the same time.' },
  ]}
/>
